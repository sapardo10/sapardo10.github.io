Caurora support

See if tonight is worth going out, then photograph the aurora and night sky with your iPhone.

Start with a steady camera

Use a tripod and the 3-second timer. Lock focus, turn on Aurora mode and choose the sky: Faint, Active or Storm. Each sets a starting ISO, shutter and white balance within what your selected lens supports. Adjust from there to suit the sky.

Stacking

An iPhone limits how long a single frame can be exposed. On a tripod, Caurora takes a series of frames and combines them on your device into one photo that gathers more light than a single frame. The app shows the number of frames and the total time it achieved. Keep the phone still for the whole capture. Stop keeps the frames taken so far; Discard keeps nothing. Stacking appears only on lenses that support it.

Why is the maximum exposure shorter than I asked for?

Exposure duration and ISO limits depend on the iPhone and the selected physical lens. Caurora shows the real maximum for each lens and tells you when a value was limited.

Focus lock

Point at something distant, such as a bright star or far light, and tap Focus lock. Caurora focuses, then holds that position for the current lens. If the saved focus is from an earlier session, Caurora asks you to re-check it, because focus can drift. If autofocus cannot settle in the dark, use the focus dial and check stars at 100% in Review.

Tonight

Tap Tonight for a verdict where you are: Go now, Maybe, or Unlikely right now, with the reason. It combines NOAA's aurora forecast, how dark the sky is, and cloud cover from Apple Weather. It is an outlook, not a guarantee. Location is optional and approximate. Without it, Tonight shows global Kp, which measures worldwide geomagnetic activity rather than what you can see from your location.

Forecast unavailable or "Cloud: Unknown"

Forecasts need a connection. If data is old, Caurora shows its age instead of a verdict; check your connection and open Tonight again. "Cloud: Unknown" means Apple Weather could not be reached or location is off; the aurora and darkness parts still work.

Camera unavailable

Open iPhone Settings → Apps → Caurora and enable Camera. Close other camera apps and reopen Caurora. An iOS simulator has no camera and cannot take photographs.

A photo did not save

Keep Caurora open. Enable permission to add photos in iPhone Settings → Apps → Caurora, return to Caurora and tap Save photo. Check available device storage. The unsaved image stays only in app memory and is lost if you quit or discard it. RAW and its processed preview are saved together as one Photos asset.

Night mode and horizon

Red night mode tints the screen red and limits brightness to protect your night vision. The horizon level uses device motion and hides when you point almost straight up.
